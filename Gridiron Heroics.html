Seahawks 64-Year-Old All-Pro Legend Passes Away – What We Know

The Seattle Seahawks announced the passing of former linebacker Fredd Young, a four-time Pro Bowler known as one of the hardest hitters in team history. He was 64 when the franchise announced the news on Wednesday,

Young played four years in Seattle, starting on special teams before anchoring the middle of the defense for three seasons. They confirmed the news of his death without releasing a cause.

Seahawks Mourn Franchise Legend Fredd Young

Seattle drafted Young in the third round of the 1984 draft out of New Mexico State. He immediately won the AFC’s Special Teams Player of the Year award as a rookie and blocked two punts, which remains the second-most in team history. 

By 1985, he took over as a starting linebacker and led the team in tackles for three consecutive years, passing 100 stops each season. His best year came in 1987 when he recorded nine sacks, five forced fumbles, four fumble recoveries and an interception. That performance earned him a first-team All-Pro selection and third place in the NFL Defensive Player of the Year voting.

In an official statement, the team wrote, “We mourn the passing of Seahawks Legend Fredd Young. A Pro Bowler in each of his four seasons with the Seahawks, Fredd’s legacy will forever be part of our franchise. Our thoughts are with his family and loved ones.”

He never forgot where he came from. After his standout years as a student-athlete at New Mexico State University, he and his wife Michelle returned to Las Cruces. They spent decades deeply rooted in the university’s athletic community. He was survived by his wife, their seven children and seven grandchildren. Like Seattle, his family has not shared the cause of his death at this moment.

He made the Seahawks’ 35th Anniversary Team in 2010 and landed at No. 27 on the franchise’s all-time top-40 fan vote in 2016. He went into the New Mexico Sports Hall of Fame in 2024 alongside fellow Seahawk Michael Sinclair. 

Reflecting on his lifelong dedication to his alma mater, Young once shared, “NMSU opened so many doors, and now I talk to student-athletes and let them know they can do what I did: make it to the NFL, build a life, and take care of their families.”

His fearsome playing style even inspired a college teammate to suggest adding the second “D” to his name. Yet, remembering his true impact, former sportscaster Kenny Mayne noted that Young was remarkably dependable and “definitely one of the players that made the Seattle defense what it was during his time.”

Following his passing, tributes have continued to pour in from across the community. So many people have shared stories about his kind and generous heart, with many calling him one of the nicest guys they ever met.
