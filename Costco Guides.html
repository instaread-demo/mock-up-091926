What Tire Brands Does Costco Sell? Full 2026 List

ByFaran Posted onSeptember 12, 2026September 12, 2026

In 2026, Costco sells five tire brands.

Michelin, BFGoodrich, Bridgestone, Firestone, and Pirelli.

Michelin anchors the wall with the widest range, BFGoodrich handles trucks and off-road, Bridgestone and Firestone cover premium down to value, and Pirelli takes the performance slot.

Goodyear, Continental, and Yokohama are not on the wall.

The first thing I noticed at my warehouse’s tire wall was not the selection. It was how short it was. The Costco tire brands you can actually buy come down to five names from three companies, which sounds thin until you see how much ground they cover.

That short list is the whole point. Costco carries a tight set of brands it will stand behind with lifetime service, so the wall feels vetted instead of endless.

The Five Costco Tire Brands

Costco’s five tire brands trace back to just three parent companies.

Michelin and BFGoodrich are both Michelin brands, Bridgestone and Firestone are both Bridgestone brands, and Pirelli is the lone Italian maker standing on its own.

Here is the part nobody mentions at the counter. The real choice is not five brands, it is three corporate families, each with a premium option and a value option.

Pick your family first, then your budget inside it, and the call gets faster.

 Michelin gets the most shelf space, the most signage, and most of the foot traffic. 

The other four fill in trucks, value, and performance around it.

  • You can see the live lineup on Costco’s tire page, which lets you filter by vehicle or size.

Michelin and BFGoodrich at Costco

Michelin is Costco’s flagship tire brand and the one most members walk in asking for. It carries the CrossClimate2 all-weather tire, the Pilot Sport performance line, and the X-ICE Snow winter tire, and it is consistently rated at the top for tread life and grip.

It is also the priciest brand on the wall, and I think the premium earns its keep for one reason.

The CrossClimate2 holds up in light snow well enough to skip the winter-tire swap most years.

BFGoodrich is the pick for trucks and SUVs, especially anything that leaves pavement. It sits under the same Michelin ownership and brings a real off-road heritage, with the All-Terrain T/A KO3, the Trail-Terrain T/A+, and the Advantage all-season in the mix.

Prices usually land under Michelin while keeping the durability. Fair warning from experience, the KO3 hums on the highway, and that noise is the trade for the off-road bite.

Bridgestone and Firestone at Costco

Bridgestone is the other premium all-rounder in the lineup, leaning toward quiet touring and all-season comfort.

Several models carry the three-peak mountain snowflake winter rating and a 60,000-mile warranty, which makes it a strong pick if you want a long-wearing tire without paying Michelin money.

Firestone is the value brand of the group, owned by Bridgestone and priced to match. It is the closest thing to a budget tire on the Costco wall and covers the basics well for commuters and light trucks.

If I had to knock one brand here, it is Firestone, and only barely.

The gap up to Bridgestone is not huge, you are on the same corporate lineage either way, and the extra spend often buys a quieter, longer-lasting tire.

This Costco tire prices guide lays out how the brands stack up on cost.

Pirelli at Costco

Pirelli is the performance and luxury specialist of the five.

Costco stocks the P Zero ultra-high-performance tire, some with run-flat technology, plus the Cinturato P7 all-season and the Scorpion line for SUVs and winter.

This is the brand to look at for a sports car or a European sedan that rolled off the line on Pirellis.

My honest take, if your car did not come on performance tires, you probably do not need them, and one of the other four is the better value.

That focus makes Pirelli the narrowest range at Costco. You get strong coverage for sporty and premium fitments and fewer everyday touring options, and a handful of Pirelli sizes now ship online only through Costco.com rather than sitting in the warehouse.

Costco Tire Installation and Warranty on Every Brand

The question I get most is whether Costco’s tires are the same ones you would buy at a tire shop. They are genuine manufacturer models, and what changes is everything Costco wraps around them.

Every set, whatever the brand, comes with free installation, a 5-year road hazard warranty, and lifetime rotation, balancing, flat repairs, and nitrogen inflation.

The manufacturer’s own mileage warranty still applies on top of all that.

The catch worth budgeting for is that installation only covers tires bought at Costco, and a TPMS service pack fee can get added at checkout.

You can price your exact fitment and book a bay on the Costco tire center site before you drive over.

One small reality of the process, they send you to wait out in the warehouse, not the service bay, while the work gets done. You get called up when the car is ready.

Tire Brands Costco Does Not Carry

The gaps matter as much as the lineup. Costco does not carry Goodyear, Continental, or Yokohama, and two of those rank among the biggest-selling names in the country.

If your heart is set on one of them, a dedicated tire retailer is your stop or you can compare costco with other options.

Costco does not sell a Kirkland Signature tire today. It offered Kirkland-branded tires built by Michelin years ago and quietly retired them, so the current wall is the five manufacturer brands and nothing house-labeled.

Availability also shifts over time. Costco has tested other brands before and adjusts the roster, so treat these five as the current lineup, not a permanent one.

How Costco Tire Selection Compares to Other Stores

Five brands looks thin next to a Discount Tire or a Tire Rack, which each list dozens. The difference is on purpose.

Costco buys direct from a few makers and stocks only models it will back with lifetime service, so you get a shorter, vetted wall instead of endless choice.

For most drivers that is plenty, because the five already span premium, value, truck, and performance. Where Costco comes up short is the edges, like a specific budget import brand, a track-only tire, or a hard-to-find size.

If that is you, a specialist with a deeper catalog wins.

Which Costco tire brand is best?

It depends on your vehicle.

Michelin is the strongest all-round pick, BFGoodrich suits trucks and off-road use, and Firestone is the value option, so match the brand to how you drive.

Are Costco tires cheaper than a tire shop?

Not always on the sticker price. What tilts the math is what comes bundled in, since free installation, a 5-year road hazard warranty, and lifetime rotation and balancing come with every set and cost extra elsewhere.

How to Choose the Right Costco Tire

Start with your vehicle and how you drive, not the brand name. A daily commuter is well served by Michelin or Bridgestone all-seasons, a truck or SUV that sees dirt points to BFGoodrich, and a sporty car leans Pirelli. Firestone is the move when budget leads.

Budget is the other deciding factor. Firestone and BFGoodrich generally sit below Michelin and Pirelli, so when two brands both fit your car, the cheaper one often makes sense.

One pattern worth knowing is that Michelin and Bridgestone tend to headline Costco’s biggest instant-savings events. Timing a set around one of those promos can close the gap to the cheaper brands.

 Not every model is stocked at every warehouse, so check the tire finder for your exact size and vehicle before you plan a visit. 

Still stuck between two sets?

Tell the counter staff your typical mileage and conditions and let them match a tire to it.

Once you have picked, booking is the same for every brand, and our guide to booking a Costco tire appointment walks through the steps.
